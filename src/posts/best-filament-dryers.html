Best filament dryers (2026): a research-based buying guide

How this guide was made: we have not tested these dryers ourselves. Everything below comes from manufacturer specs, retailer listings, and published independent reviews, all linked at the bottom. Prices were checked on October 28, 2026 and change often.

Why dry filament?

Most 3D-printing filaments absorb moisture from the air. PETG, ABS, TPU, PC and nylon absorb more than PLA does, and wet filament can cause stringing and rough surfaces [5][7].

What independent testers found

  • Temperature accuracy (3D Print Beginner, Feb 2024) [5]: the SUNLU S4 read between −2 °C and +2 °C of its set temperature. The Creality Space Pi read 5–14 °C hotter than set; for example, it measured 81 °C at a 70 °C setting. The tester warned this could be a risk for PLA, TPU and PETG. These are one reviewer's results, with a probe near the top of each unit, using review units supplied by the manufacturers.
  • Power draw (same test) [5]: about 120 W peak for the Space Pi and about 295 W for the S4.
  • Sealing [5]: that tester said neither the Space Pi nor the S4 held low humidity overnight once switched off, so neither works well as a storage box.
  • Sovol SH03 [7]: in a sponsored-channel video (the unit was free, with no other payment), it reached about 84–85 °C with the lid sealed. Readings were lower when a gap was left for the sensor wire. Its rollers were placed wide apart, which made spools turn less smoothly, and no desiccant came in the box.
  • Display accuracy: one Creality forum user reported that an SH03's humidity readings didn't match an external hygrometer after a 16 h run at 80 °C [9]. That is a single anecdote.

The picks in detail

SUNLU S4: best for drying several spools

Four spools, eight filament outlets, three fans, and auto-humidity mode [2][3]. It had the best temperature accuracy in the one head-to-head test we found [5]. Downsides: it's large (about 460 × 220 × 310 mm) and power-hungry, and the reviewer found it noisy and poorly sealed [5].

Check SUNLU S4 price

Sovol SH03: best for high-temperature filament

It is the only dryer here that goes past 70 °C, with an 85 °C maximum, which matters for nylon and similar materials. It has two chambers you can run independently and vents that open automatically [4]. Downsides from one review: filament can only exit upward, no desiccant is included, and the spool rollers are stiff [7].

Check Sovol SH03 price

Creality Space Pi: compact single-spool pick

A cheap, small unit with a touchscreen and 12 material presets [1]. The independent test found it overshoots its set temperature, by up to 14 °C [5]. If you buy one, consider setting it lower than the preset for PLA. That tip comes from the reviewer's results; we have not checked it ourselves.

Check Creality Space Pi price

Polymaker PolyDryer: dry + store

One dock dries whichever sealed box is sitting on it. Extra boxes listed at $29.99 in one review [10]. There's no set-temperature control, only 3 power levels [6]. It was listed as unavailable on Polymaker's store when we checked.
